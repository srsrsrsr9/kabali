Where this actually turns

The language rule is the constraint, not the fees and not the commute

Your budget clears most of the schools in your tabs, and almost all of them are within a 20‑minute bus ride of IndiQube ETA. Neither of those is what will decide this. Two rules that both changed recently do:

CBSE now requires three languages in Classes 9–10

Circular Acad‑33/2026, effective 1 July 2026, makes R1, R2 and R3 compulsory from Class IX, with at least two of the three being native Indian languages. Since English is the medium and occupies the non‑native slot in a private English‑medium school, R2 and R3 must both be Indian languages. Only R1 and R2 are examined by the Board in Class X; R3 is assessed internally by the school and still appears on the CBSE certificate.

Karnataka requires Kannada as the first or second language

Under the Kannada Language Learning Act 2015 and the NOC rules the state began enforcing from 2024–25, CBSE and ICSE schools in Karnataka must teach Kannada as first or second language — not third — for Classes 1 to 10. The state used to accept it in the third slot and stopped. A 2023 PIL against this is still pending in the Karnataka High Court, with no final verdict; enforcement runs through the school's NOC, so it is the school, not you, that carries the risk.

Put together: English + Kannada + one more is the combination a Bengaluru CBSE school offers by default. Telugu is not the obstacle at the Board — CBSE runs full Class 9–10 syllabi and board papers for Telugu under codes 007 (Andhra Pradesh) and 089 (Telangana). The obstacle is that almost no private school in Bengaluru staffs it. Government schools do; private ones offer Kannada, Hindi, Sanskrit and sometimes French.

That sounds worse than it is, and the reason is the R3 change. A school that would never staff Telugu as a board‑examined subject can plausibly accept it as R3, because R3 carries no Board paper for your elder child's batch — assessment is entirely school‑based, and CBSE has explicitly told schools to make do with shared teachers and Class 6 textbooks while regional‑language capacity catches up. That is the door to push on.

The relief to cite by name

Karnataka's own framing provides that a student who has taken a particular language combination at the middle stage may continue it on reaching Class 9. Your elder child is finishing Grade 8 on Telugu and Hindi right now — that is precisely the middle‑stage combination the relief contemplates. Raise it explicitly; a principal who has heard it before will handle it differently from one hearing a parent ask for a favour.

The two children need different answers

Grade 5 is a different problem, and the timing flips it

GRADE 9 · CLASS X BOARD 2029

R3 is internal. Optimise for the Board aggregate.

The first Class X Board exam in R3 is for the cohort that entered Class 6 in 2026–27 — Class X in 2030–31. Your elder child sits the Board a year or two before that, so R3 stays school‑assessed for them. Every language argument above is about protecting the two subjects that do count. Fight for Hindi or Telugu at R2; treat R3 as free.

Worth confirming in writing with the school, since it rests on the transitional schedule rather than a rule stated for this specific batch.

GRADE 5 · CLASS X BOARD 2032–33

R3 will be examined. Kannada is unavoidable — and fine.

Two things follow. First, Kannada as first or second language is mandatory up to Class 8, so this child will do Kannada — but with five years of runway before anything is graded by the Board, that is genuinely learnable and worth having in Bengaluru. Don't spend negotiating capital fighting it.

Second, and less obvious: by 2032–33 R3 is a Board subject. If Telugu never enters this child's school record, it never appears on the certificate. Aim to get Telugu into R3 from Class 6 onward — for a native speaker that turns the third language from a burden into the easiest paper on the sheet.

The practical read: your realistic school offer is English + Kannada + Hindi for the younger child, with Telugu kept alive at home, and a school that will revisit R3 at Class 6. For the elder, push hard for Telugu in any slot. It is entirely reasonable for the two children to end up on different combinations inside the same school.

Ask these in this order, on every call

The nine questions

Front desks will give you fee sheets and campus tours. None of that resolves your actual problem. Lead with the language question and name the circular — it changes who you get transferred to.

  1. Which languages do you offer as R1, R2 and R3 for Class IX under Circular Acad‑33/2026?If they don't know the circular, ask for the academic coordinator.
  2. Are you registered on OASIS to offer Telugu — subject code 007 or 089?This is a yes/no fact about the school, not an opinion. It settles Configuration 2 immediately.
  3. If Telugu isn't available as R2, will you take it as R3 with internal school-based assessment?Point out there is no Class X Board paper for R3 for this batch. This is the ask most likely to succeed.
  4. Your NOC requires Kannada as first or second language. How do you handle a Class 9 lateral entrant from another state who has never studied Kannada?Ask what they have actually done for previous such students — not what the policy says.
  5. Is the combination locked at admission, and can it be changed before you upload Class IX registration to CBSE?Get the deadline date. That is your real decision point.
  6. Do you run Classes XI and XII, and in which streams?For your Grade 9 child this is close to disqualifying if the answer is no.
  7. Are there Class IX seats for 2027–28 at all, is there an entrance test, and on what date?
  8. Does your bus cover [your rental zone]? What is the pickup time and the one-way ride time?Ask for ride time, not distance. On ORR they are unrelated.
  9. What is the full annual outgo — tuition, admission, transport, books, uniform, and any building or development fee?Ask for it as one number per child per year, in writing.

Housing, honestly

The straight version: ₹30–35k all‑in does not get you a 3BHK in a gated community anywhere in this corridor. Those are running ₹50–60k in Kundalahalli, Mahadevapura and Hoodi, and higher in the newer projects. What ₹30–35k does get you, reliably, is a 2BHK — either in an older apartment society or as an independent builder floor — in every zone below.

So the useful reframing is: you almost certainly do not need to settle for a 1BHK. A builder-floor 2BHK in Kaggadasapura or Hoodi sits at ₹26–30k, which is inside your preferred number, not your stretch. With a Class 9 board-track student and a Grade 5 child sharing the same space for two years, the second room is worth more than the address is.

The deposit rule changed in your favour

Bengaluru's 10‑month deposit convention is no longer enforceable. The Karnataka Rent (Amendment) Act, 2025 — gazetted 8 January 2026 — caps residential security deposits at two months' rent, on the Model Tenancy Act framework. At ₹32,000 rent that is ₹64,000 rather than ₹3.2 lakh.

Expect landlords and brokers in this corridor to quote 10 months anyway. Anything above two months is not enforceable in a Rent Tribunal — say so, and be ready to walk. Register the agreement digitally on Kaveri 2.0; skipping it carries a ₹5,000 penalty and leaves you without the protection.

Hold the metro in mind before you sign

The Namma Metro Blue Line along ORR — Silk Board to KR Puram, with a station at Doddanekkundi — is targeted for December 2026, after slipping from a June 2026 date. Assume further slippage; the corridor has a record.

The hedge is cheap: prefer a flat within walking distance of Doddanekkundi, Kundalahalli or Mahadevapura station, on a 11‑month lease rather than a longer one. If the line opens on your first renewal, your commute problem disappears and rents in those pockets rise — you want to already be inside that, not chasing it.

You are at the start of the window, not ahead of it

What happens when

Bengaluru CBSE admissions for 2027–28 open between roughly late August and December 2026 and run first‑come until seats fill. Vagdevi Vilas opens on Ganesha Chaturthi; Presidency East has opened on 1 October. Class 9 has far fewer seats than the entry grades and they go early.

September 2026 — now

Call the three priority schools with the nine questions. Language answer first; do not let the conversation become a fee conversation. Log every answer in one sheet so you can compare.

October 2026

Registrations open at most schools. Apply broadly — application fees are small relative to the cost of having no Class 9 seat. Get any language accommodation confirmed by email, not verbally.

November – December 2026

Entrance tests and parent interactions. Shortlist to three. Visit at 8am on a school day and ride the actual bus route from your candidate rental zone — that number is the one that governs your family's mornings for four years.

December 2026

Metro Blue Line target date. Whatever its status by then, you will know enough to weight Zone A against Zone C. Pick your rental zone here — after the school is decided, not before.

January – February 2027

Accept an offer and pay. Request the Transfer Certificate from the current school — this is the step that most often runs late for interstate moves, so start it early. Confirm the CBSE Class IX registration deadline, which is when the language combination stops being changeable.

February – March 2027

Sign the lease. Two months' deposit, 11‑month term, registered on Kaveri 2.0. Time possession for a few weeks before the session starts, not the same week.

April – June 2027

Session begins — April at most schools in your list, June at a few. Confirm which, because it moves your entire move date by two months.
